Madurai had been on our list for a long time, and when we finally went, the plan was simple. We were going to eat. The Vande Bharat, 20672 and 20671, took us there and back, with a little over 50 hours in between. Our room at [Gopuram Grand](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Gopuram+Grand/@9.9197268,78.1177974,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c5a9049a68ab:0x8a8291547438e713!8m2!3d9.9197268!4d78.1177974) was hardly 50 metres from the Meenakshi Amman Temple’s west gate, so the temple was on our doorstep and the food list was in our pocket. My [Google Maps list of Madurai](https://www.google.com/maps/%409.9220756,78.1130602,14.83z/data=!4m3!11m2!2s4jqQ-Zy2IAeMAlx7mgwqwfX1H2Acyw!3e3) has every place in this post.

![Signboards from Gopu Iyengar, Nagapatnam and Janani](/img/scenes/madurai-1.jpg)

Madurai did not let us just eat, though. The temple sits at the centre of everything, and we kept getting pulled back towards it, so the meals ended up fitting between the sights, and the sights between the meals. *If you are here only for the food, [skip straight to it](#what-we-came-for).*

## Between meals

We went to the Meenakshi Amman Temple early in the morning, when the crowd was thin. Phones and sandals are not allowed inside, so we left everything in the room and walked in with empty hands. The towers are covered in painted figures, and each one has its own shape, since they were built at different times. At night they look different again. We went around the temple several times over the two days, inside and outside, and every time we found something to stop and look up at.

![The temple tower, lit up at night](/img/scenes/madurai-2.jpg "split")
![A tower framed by a gateway](/img/scenes/madurai-3.jpg "split")

![A sketch of the [White Nandi](https://maps.app.goo.gl/gQYkV1fvQo4s2YER7)](/img/scenes/madurai-4.jpg)

A 20-minute walk from the temple brought us to the Thirumalai Nayakkar Mahal, what remains of a much larger palace from the 1600s. There is hardly any documentation to go with it, so the courtyard does the talking, with its rows of arches and a mix of cultures stitched into one design. We meant it to be a quick visit, but the hours disappeared.

![The courtyard of the Mahal](/img/scenes/madurai-5.jpg)
![A sketch of the Thirumalai Nayakkar Mahal](/img/scenes/madurai-6.jpg)

St. Mary’s Cathedral Shrine is just two minutes from the Mahal. Its architecture is more Roman than that of most churches we have seen, with bright Dravidian shades, and it is narrower in proportion.

![Roman arches in bright Dravidian shades, St. Mary’s](/img/scenes/madurai-7.jpg)

We ended up watching a movie at [Thanga Regal](https://in.bookmyshow.com/cinemas/madurai/thanga-regal-cinema-dolby-atmos-laser-madurai/buytickets/THRM), a plan that came together on the spot. It started as the Victoria Edward Town Hall in 1912, and after years as Regal Talkies it has been renovated with its old charm intact. The screen is not very big, but the Dolby Atmos sound made the whole experience great.

![Thanga Regal, the old Town Hall turned cinema](/img/scenes/madurai-8.jpg)

We also stopped at the Madurai Bazar Post Office, an old building close to the temple. The staff couldn’t have been nicer.

![A sketch of Madurai Bazar Post Office](/img/scenes/madurai-9.jpg)

## What we came for

### Tiffin and coffee

We had most of our coffee at [N.G Sri Meenakshi Coffee Bar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=N.G+Sri+Meenakshi+Coffee+Bar+Madurai), on the side closer to the west tower. It sits on both sides of the road, one side for meals and the other for coffee, and we stayed on the coffee side. The coffee is [Narasu’s](https://narasuscoffee.in), a must-have, and by evening the same side also serves snacks, butter buns and sweets. It was crowded every time we went.

![The crowd outside N.G Sri Meenakshi Coffee Bar](/img/scenes/madurai-10.jpg)

[Gopu Iyengar Tiffin Center](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Gopu+Iyengar+Tiffin+Center+Madurai) is one of those Madurai names that come with a legacy, a landmark in a corner close to the temple. It is a go-to for breakfast and just as good for evening snacks, with the green chutney and the *vellai appam* (a bit like a vada made with rice, not to be confused with the Kerala *vellappam*). The space is tiny and always full, with a seating system of its own. Older hands run the place, and the staff chalk each order on a slate before it goes into a KOT (kitchen order ticket), the old way and the new way side by side. Through all the chaos, they make sure everyone eats well and leaves happy.

![Evening snacks with green chutney](/img/scenes/madurai-11.jpg "split")
![Fritters and chutneys on a banana leaf](/img/scenes/madurai-12.jpg "split")

One evening, roaming around North Chithirai Road, we stumbled on a roadside shop selling Sourashtra poori, an evening snack made with Indian coral tree leaves. It tasted different from everything else we had.

### Biriyani and mutton

[Vadakku Masi Vithi Konar Soup Kadai](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Vadakku+Masi+Vithi+Konar+Soup+Kadai+Madurai) was our top spot for mutton, an old and rustic place where everything came out fresh. The biriyani, *curry dosai*, *pichipotta chicken* and *mutton sukka* were all great. [Simmakkal Konar Kadai](https://www.google.com/maps/place/SIMMAKKAL+KONAR+KADAI/@9.9177179,78.1115457,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c554e895d611:0xbed80027c04f13b8!8m2!3d9.9177179!4d78.1115457) is another matter. It is a franchise, and apart from the name it shares nothing with this shop, so don’t mix them up. Even in a city full of *Konar kadais*, this one earned its place.

![Rice at Vadakku Masi Vithi Konar Soup Kadai](/img/scenes/madurai-13.jpg "split")
![Certificate of Quality 😉](/img/scenes/madurai-14.jpg "split")

At [Ashok Evening Mutton Stall](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Ashok+evening+mutton+stall/@9.9180596,78.1243278,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c59c82e225ab:0xa95b1e2e87505eb3!8m2!3d9.9180596!4d78.1243278) we had porotta with *mutton chukka*. We got there late, and the bun porotta had already sold out. [Kumar Mess](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Kumar+Mess/@9.9319303,78.1338357,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c5be28fafbf3:0xe10ea207887d0ae7!8m2!3d9.9319303!4d78.1338357) was a different story. We had biriyani, *mutton chukka* and *pichipotta chicken* there, and nothing stood out. It felt a little alien next to everything else we ate in Madurai.

The meal at [New Janani Mess](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=New+Janani+Mess+Madurai) began with a long walk, twenty to twenty-five minutes through the market, with the hunger getting louder at every turn. The porotta came on a banana leaf with four *salnas*, a regular one that is spicy enough to start with, one with tomato and garlic, one with coriander that they also use for their quail curry, and a spicy one made for the egg. The *pichipotta chicken* was excellent. Then came the *kalaki omelette*, half-cooked, super fluffy and spicy, and everything else on the table went quiet. It ruins every other egg you will eat afterwards.

![Porotta, salnas and pichipotta chicken at Janani](/img/scenes/madurai-15.jpg)

Of all the *mutton chukka* we had, Janani’s was the best, with Vadakku Masi very close, and Ashok third for its rich gingelly oil flavour. Simmakkal Konar Kadai served a very garlicky version, while Kumar Mess was an ordinary mutton curry and not much of a *chukka*.

### The sweet side

Madurai has a sweet tooth, and almost every shop offers a small sample. [Aathi Kalathu Original Nei Mittai Kadai](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Aathi+Kalathu+Original+Nei+Mittai+Kadai/@9.9198822,78.1216767,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c584b70198bd:0x68767500e1ad47e4!8m2!3d9.9198822!4d78.1216767) had a great halwa and warm service, though we missed their famous *Kizhangu Pottalam*, a paper packet of potato masala. For *nei mittai*, [Nagapatnam Halwa Shop](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Nagapatnam+Halwa+Shop/@9.9195829,78.1179012,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c5846d188de5:0xa64577dd656f0b08!8m2!3d9.9195829!4d78.1179012), next to the west gate, won us over. [Prema Vilas Halwa Shop](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Prema+Vilas+Halwa+Shop,+Town+Hall+Rd,+Madurai+Main,+Madurai,+Tamil+Nadu+625001/@9.9179762,78.111828,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x3b00c580b3160149:0xbe447a9406095689!8m2!3d9.9179762!4d78.111828) on Town Hall Road is the other famous name, but it left us a little disappointed. One *jigarthanda* from the outlet nearest the temple cooled it all down.

## Before you go

Stay close to the temple. Phones and sandals are not allowed inside the temple, so leave them at the hotel. Autos are brutal, even on Uber, so walk as much as you can. Restaurants keep their own timings, and some close for lunch or on weekends. Some do not take UPI or cards either, so check before you go and carry cash. If you plan to buy handloom sarees, go to the government or co-op shops.

![Shops around the temple](/img/scenes/madurai-18.jpg "split")
![A tower of painted figures, up close](/img/scenes/madurai-19.jpg "split")

We came to Madurai for the food, and somewhere between the chalk slate at Gopu Iyengar, the crowd at N.G and a plate of *kalaki omelette*, we fell for the city. Half the list is still waiting, so we will be back.
