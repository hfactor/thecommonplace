Boiling water in the bottom chamber builds steam pressure and pushes up through the grounds into the top collector. The result is rich and concentrated, a little cloudy, closer to espresso than filter coffee. [James Hoffmann has a good breakdown of the process](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BfDqO0f8080) if you want the details.

Beans that have worked well: Julien Peak Forest, Wanderoo.

| | |
|---|---|
| Coffee | 12.5g |
| Water | 140ml, for a Bialetti 3-cup pot |
| Grind | Fine, like table salt |
| Time | 2 minutes 30 seconds |
| Target | 100ml+ |
| Roast | Medium-dark to dark |

1. Clean and dry the chambers.
2. Fill the bottom chamber with hot water up to the safety valve.
3. Fill the basket with coffee, level it gently, don't tamp.
4. Wipe loose grounds off the rim and screw the pot together tightly.
5. Set it on medium-low heat.
6. When coffee starts to emerge, drop the heat to the lowest setting.
7. Take it off the stove as soon as the stream turns light blonde.
8. Cool the bottom chamber under a cold tap, or wrap it in a wet towel, to stop extraction.
9. Serve right away. Dilute with hot water if it's too strong.
