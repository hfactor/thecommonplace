Start with a kettle. A [gooseneck kettle](https://somethingsbrewing.in/products/budan-electric-temperature-control-kettle-1l-capacity) with temperature control gives you the most control over your brew. If you don’t have one, use a regular kettle with an external [thermometer](https://link.amazon/B07JUZvp6) and a [drip pot.](https://link.amazon/B00N6YeaL)

A grinder is worth having if you’re brewing with whole beans. Manual grinders are a good choice unless you’re making a lot of coffee. Good options range from around ₹4,000 to ₹30,000, including the [AAshonee Classic](https://link.amazon/B01cuJzrm) Classic, [Timemore C2S](https://link.amazon/B05Ao1e6d), [1zpresso J Ultra](https://www.benkibrewingtools.com/collections/1zpresso-1/products/1zpresso-j-ultra-manual-coffee-grinder), and [Comandante C40 MK4](https://brewinggadgets.in/products/comandante-mk4-nitro-blade-coffee-grinder-alpine-lagoon-copy-3). If you prefer electric, the [Baratza Encore](https://brewinggadgets.in/products/baratza-encore-esp-black) is a good option. 

A [scale](https://somethingsbrewing.in/products/hario-drip-scale-for-espresso-and-manual-brewing-vstn-2000b) is also useful for measuring coffee and water consistently. Most coffee scales have a timer, but your phone works too. Depending on your brewing method, you’ll also need paper filters.
