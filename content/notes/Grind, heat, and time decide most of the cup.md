---
title: "Grind, heat, and time decide most of the cup"
date: 2026-02-20
categories: ["Manual Brewing"]
categories_weight: 3
---
Water pulls compounds out of ground coffee, some quickly, some slowly. Most of what you taste comes down to a handful of variables you're already touching without thinking about it.

**Grind size.** Finer grounds slow the water down and let it extract more; coarser grounds let water through fast and extract less. Very fine feels like flour, fine like table salt, medium like sand, coarse like rough sea salt.

**Water temperature.** Hotter water extracts faster. A reasonable starting range is 90°C to 96°C.

**Coffee dose and water amount.** How much dry coffee you use, and how much water you pour against it. The ratio between the two is most of what separates a strong cup from a weak one.

**Contact time.** How long the coffee and water actually spend together.

**Agitation.** Stirring or swirling speeds extraction up, sometimes usefully, sometimes not.
