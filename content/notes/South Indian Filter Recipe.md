Ground coffee sits in the upper chamber of a metal two-piece filter. Hot water drips slowly through the bed into the lower chamber over about 15 minutes, giving a thick, heavy, concentrated decoction.

Commercial powders worth trying: Narasu's Anandam, a 70-30 coffee-chicory blend, or Cothas Ground Coffee.

| | |
|---|---|
| Coffee | 22 to 28g, medium-dark to dark roast, 10 to 40% chicory |
| Water | 100 to 120g |
| Time | 15 minutes |

1. Add the powder to the upper chamber, shake gently to level it.
2. Rest the plunger disk on top. Don't tamp hard, just let it sit. It spreads the water evenly and stops dry spots forming.
3. Set the upper chamber on the lower collector.
4. Add 30ml hot water, wait 1 minute.
5. Pour the rest slowly over the disk, without disturbing the bed underneath.
6. Cover and let it drip undisturbed for 15 minutes.
7. Pour out the decoction. Traditionally mixed with hot milk and sugar.

A couple of things worth trying: if making milk coffee, put a little sugar (about 3g) directly on the dry powder before pouring water, it seems to bring out the chicory's sweetness. And if the filter is copper, heating the upper chamber directly over the stove now and then helps clear the tiny holes and keeps extraction even.
